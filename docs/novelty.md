# What is new here

DEWR (2026) has already mapped Massenkoff and McCrory's (2026) usage-based "observed exposure" onto ANZSCO unit groups, as an alternative to JSA's automation scores in a model of employment. It does not examine earnings. Building on that mapping, this project asks a descriptive question: across Australia's earnings distribution, where does measured AI use run ahead of or behind measured AI potential?

It adds:

1. The usage-potential gap by employment-weighted earnings decile, against JSA and Eloundou et al., and against AIOE in aggregate only.
2. An extensive margin (linked US occupations with any measured use) and intensity.
3. WA against the nation, which reflects occupation mix by construction.
4. Results for women and men.

Every result is rerun under each crosswalk scheme, and again on non-diffuse units only.

e61 (22 May 2026) argued for moving beyond exposure metrics. This project moves only from "could be used" to "is being used", not to labour-market outcomes. e61's revenue scenarios (7 August 2026) assume any AI wage change is equal across the income distribution. This project describes, without forecasting, how usage-based exposure is spread across earnings deciles.

No Australian occupational AI use is measured. The scores are US task-level Claude.ai use by self-selected users of one product.
