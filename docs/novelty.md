# What is new here

DEWR (2026) has already mapped Massenkoff and McCrory's (2026) usage-based "observed exposure" onto ANZSCO unit groups, as an alternative to JSA's automation scores in a model of employment. Demirev (2026) maps an earlier Anthropic usage score to ANZSCO, through three-digit ISCO, to study Australian job postings. Neither examines earnings. In the US, Levkoff (2026) compares observed and capability-based exposure and reports a wage-quintile gradient. This project asks a descriptive Australian question: across the earnings distribution, where does measured AI use run ahead of or behind measured AI potential?

It adds:

1. the usage-potential gap by employment-weighted earnings decile, against JSA and Eloundou et al., and AIOE in aggregate only;
2. an extensive margin (any measured use among linked US occupations) and intensity;
3. WA against the nation, which reflects occupation mix by construction, and results for women and men;
4. a graded crosswalk under four weighting schemes, with every specification reported.

e61 (22 May 2026) argued for moving beyond exposure metrics. This project moves only from "could be used" to "is being used", not to labour-market outcomes. e61's revenue scenarios (7 August 2026) assume any AI wage change is equal across the income distribution. This project describes, without forecasting, how usage-based exposure is spread across earnings deciles.

No Australian occupational AI use is measured. The scores are task-level Claude.ai and first-party API use by self-selected users of one provider, classified to US occupational tasks.
